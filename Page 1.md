# Research Strategy / Technical Approach

## 1. Technical Rationale and System Architecture

The DOE Genesis Mission identifies **AI for Scientific Reasoning** as a cross-cutting need for next-generation discovery workflows. Topic 19 states that current large language models, despite their success in natural-language processing, remain limited on complex scientific tasks requiring deep analytical and mathematical reasoning, hypothesis generation from complex datasets, experiment design, and rigorous, verifiable, multimodal reasoning. The FOA therefore calls for a new class of AI systems specifically architected for scientific reasoning: systems built around **powerful base LLMs**, integrated with other foundation models and external scientific tools, and capable of functioning as sophisticated agents in a **closed-loop discovery engine**. Within this topic, Focus Area **19C – Composable and Modular Foundation Models** emphasizes the development of **strong base LLMs designed for flexible integration with other specialized foundation models and/or tools to enable complex, cross-domain reasoning**. Phase I projects are expected to define a **clear, tangible research workflow** and provide **quantitative evidence of AI advantage** or a credible trajectory toward transformative scientific capability.

Our proposed project addresses this need through a **sovereign, multi-agent, composable scientific foundation model architecture** for viral protein reasoning. The central premise is that viral biology provides an unusually demanding but well-bounded testbed for 19C. Scientifically meaningful reasoning about viral emergence, host adaptation, and immune escape requires synthesis across heterogeneous representations: amino-acid sequence, biomolecular structure, evolutionary history, quantitative statistical evidence, and prior mechanistic knowledge from literature and databases. Existing approaches typically fail in one of two ways. General LLMs can describe biology fluently, but they do not reliably perform mechanistic or tool-grounded scientific reasoning. Specialized biological models, by contrast, may perform well on narrow tasks, but they are usually isolated, difficult to combine, and poorly integrated with broader workflows for hypothesis formation, evidence reconciliation, and uncertainty-aware decision support. Our technical approach is to close this gap by treating composability not as an afterthought, but as the core design principle of the entire system. 

The resulting system, **SOVEREIGN-VIROFM**, is organized around four tightly coupled layers. At the top is a **governed base scientific LLM** that serves as the reasoning and orchestration core. This model will interpret scientific questions, decompose them into analyzable subtasks, route those subtasks to specialized agents and tools, integrate intermediate outputs, and produce an auditable final reasoning trace. In the second layer are **specialized scientific agents**, each responsible for a distinct reasoning regime. The most important of these is a **from-scratch Viral Protein Foundation Agent**, trained on curated viral protein corpora to learn domain-specific representations of viral sequence grammar, functional constraint, mutation context, and evolutionary regularity. Additional agents include a **Structure Reasoning Agent**, an **Evolutionary Dynamics Agent**, a **Quantitative Inference Agent**, and an **Evidence Agent** for literature and knowledge-resource integration. The third layer is a standardized **model-and-tool interface layer**, which enables adapters, wrappers, metadata exchange, provenance logging, and uncertainty propagation across heterogeneous computational components. The fourth layer is the **closed-loop scientific workflow layer**, in which the system uses the coordinated outputs of the agents to generate hypotheses, rank candidate explanations, identify uncertainty, and recommend follow-on analyses. This architecture directly instantiates the FOA’s vision of base LLMs combined with specialized models and external tools in agentic discovery workflows. 

A defining feature of this project is the concept of a **sovereign scientific model stack**. In this proposal, “sovereign” does not refer merely to model ownership; it refers to a governed end-to-end computational pipeline in which training data, model weights, evaluation artifacts, provenance records, and deployment pathways are auditable, reproducible, and suitable for trusted execution in U.S.-controlled scientific computing environments. This is important technically and scientifically. A modular scientific AI system can only become a reliable part of the discovery process if its reasoning steps, evidence sources, intermediate artifacts, and uncertainty characteristics can be inspected and reproduced. This governance-oriented design also aligns well with the broader Genesis Mission platform philosophy, which emphasizes the development of reusable AI models, workflows, and artifacts that can be integrated into larger national scientific AI capabilities such as the American Science Cloud and related model ecosystems. 

The overall technical hypothesis guiding this work is that **a governed base scientific LLM, when coupled through a multi-agent orchestration layer to a viral protein foundation model and other scientific reasoning tools, will produce superior cross-domain viral reasoning compared with non-composable baselines**. We expect the gains to appear not only in prediction-oriented measures, but also in workflow-level metrics that are especially relevant to Topic 19: reasoning coherence, evidence traceability, uncertainty calibration, and time-to-hypothesis. This is a critical distinction. Our goal is not to build a virus-specific chatbot, nor simply to train a better protein embedding model. Our goal is to demonstrate a prototype architecture in which scientific AI behaves as a modular reasoning system that can integrate diverse models and tools in a controlled, auditable, and extensible manner. That framing is precisely what makes the project responsive to 19C rather than a conventional bioinformatics application.

In Phase I, we will focus on a sharply bounded proof of concept consistent with the Genesis Mission phased structure. The FOA states that Phase I should demonstrate a clear research workflow and quantitatively assess whether the proposed approach is on a trajectory toward transformative scientific capability. Accordingly, we will not attempt to build a fully scaled universal platform in the initial nine-month effort. Instead, we will construct a working prototype centered on two representative viral science workflows: **antigenic escape assessment** and **cross-species adaptation analysis**. These tasks are scientifically meaningful, computationally heterogeneous, and well suited for evaluating the value of composable reasoning. They require coordinated use of sequence-derived signals, structural interpretation, evolutionary plausibility analysis, quantitative ranking, and literature-grounded evidence checks. By designing the system around these workflows, we can directly test whether a multi-agent architecture creates measurable scientific value beyond single-model baselines or manually assembled tool chains.

Our technical approach on this first page is therefore structured around one core principle: **composability must be engineered at the architecture level, not merely approximated by chaining together unrelated models after the fact**. The base LLM will be trained or adapted specifically for scientific planning, tool-aware reasoning, and evidence synthesis. The viral protein foundation model will be developed specifically as a reusable domain module, not as a narrow downstream classifier. The specialized agents will communicate through defined schemas that preserve provenance and confidence information. And the workflow as a whole will be evaluated by criteria that reflect the FOA’s definition of AI advantage in scientific discovery. In the sections that follow, we detail the components of the architecture, the training and integration strategy for each agent, and the evaluation plan that will determine whether this composable, sovereign, multi-agent foundation model system provides a credible path toward transformative scientific reasoning in DOE-relevant AI workflows.
